## 3. Origin story

### Internal canonical story

People & Places was founded in 2021 by Isaac Yeboah and Evans Yirenkyi, two
Ghanaians who knew that the Ghana they experienced every day was richer and
more varied than the version many people saw from outside the country.

When they shared photographs and videos from their travels around Ghana,
people often responded with surprise: “Does Ghana really have places like
this?” and “I never knew Ghana looked like this.” Kojo encountered the same
gap while hosting international travellers. Guests were repeatedly surprised
by Ghana's modern life, landscapes, culture and hospitality.

Those reactions revealed the problem People & Places was created to address:
Ghana, and Africa more broadly, was still being understood through incomplete
images and inherited stereotypes.

People & Places began as a way to tell a fuller story through photography,
film and first-hand travel. The company now creates experiences that allow
guests to encounter Ghana for themselves: through its people, food, history,
craft, landscapes and everyday rhythms.

The purpose is not simply to show that Ghana is beautiful. It is to replace
distance with understanding and turn a visit into a lasting personal
connection.

### Short public version

People & Places was founded in 2021 by two Ghanaians who kept hearing the same
question whenever they shared images from home: “I never knew Ghana looked
like this.”

They created People & Places to tell a fuller story—first through photography
and film, and then through hosted experiences that bring guests into Ghana's
culture, history, food, landscapes and everyday life.

## 4. The problem we exist to solve

### Cultural problem

Too many perceptions of Ghana and Africa are still shaped by narrow,
outdated or one-sided representations.

### Traveller problem

Visitors may know which landmarks to see, but not how to understand what they
are seeing, who to trust, how to move through Ghana confidently or how to form
meaningful connections while they are here.

### Category problem

Many travel companies sell similar itineraries using interchangeable language:
authentic, unforgettable, hidden gems, local guides and stress-free travel.
These promises are easy to make and difficult for a visitor to evaluate.

### People & Places response

We combine Ghanaian perspective, thoughtful hosting, cultural context,
storytelling and dependable planning so that guests can experience Ghana with
greater confidence and connection.

## 5. Mission

To help people experience Ghana through its people, culture, history, food,
landscapes and everyday life—and leave with a fuller understanding of the
country than the one they arrived with.

## 6. Vision

To become the most trusted and human way to experience Ghana, while helping
shape a richer and more accurate global story about the country and the
continent.

## 7. Positioning

### Recommended positioning statement

For curious travellers who want to feel Ghana rather than simply see it,
People & Places is a Ghanaian-founded travel company that brings the country
to life through local perspective, cultural context and personal hosting.
Unlike itinerary-led tour operators, we create experiences that feel less like
being shown around and more like being properly welcomed.

### Positioning shorthand

People & Places introduces travellers to Ghana through the people, stories
and everyday moments that bring the country to life.

### Public brand presentation

**People & Places**

The previously proposed descriptor “Ghanaian-hosted journeys” has been
rejected because it sounds like generic travel-category language. Do not
replace it merely to fill the space beneath the name. The homepage should use
specific hero copy to explain what People & Places does and why its founders'
lived relationship with Ghana matters. “People & Places Tours” may remain the
registered or Google listing name while the customer-facing brand uses
“People & Places.”

“Journey/journeys” is not approved as the brand's default customer-facing
noun. Use plain, specific language while the naming system for tours, visits
and other offers is developed.

### Competitive territory

People & Places should own **Ghana through human connection**.

The brand should not try to own the word “authentic.” Authenticity is an
expected standard, not a distinctive position. The evidence of authenticity
should come from named people, lived experience, cultural specificity and
real guest stories.

## 8. Audience strategy

The homepage will lead with the African diaspora while remaining explicitly
welcoming to international travellers. Audience priority guides the homepage
story; it does not prevent other customers from booking.

### Primary audience: The Meaningful Returner

Black Americans and members of the African diaspora, primarily from the
United States, with additional audiences in Canada, the United Kingdom and
Europe.

**Indicative life stage:** Primarily 28–55, while remaining inclusive of
younger guests, older guests and multigenerational families.

**Travel modes:**

- Solo travel.
- Couples.
- Families and multigenerational groups.
- Friends celebrating a milestone.
- Diaspora community and affinity groups.

**What brings them to Ghana:**

- A desire to reconnect with African heritage.
- A visit to Cape Coast, Elmina, Assin Manso or other sites of memory.
- A naming ceremony, festival or cultural experience.
- A birthday, anniversary, reunion or personal turning point.
- Curiosity about contemporary Ghana beyond inherited media narratives.
- A wish to experience food, music, art, nature and everyday hospitality.

**What they fear:**

- Choosing an unreliable operator.
- Feeling unsafe or unsupported.
- Being reduced to a tourist transaction.
- Receiving a superficial or overly commercial cultural experience.
- Poor transportation, communication or planning.
- Paying a premium without receiving personal care.
- Feeling emotionally unprepared for heritage sites.

**What they value:**

- Cultural knowledge and historical context.
- Respectful, emotionally intelligent hosting.
- Safety, reliability and clear communication.
- Flexibility and personalization.
- Comfort without cultural distance.
- Opportunities for genuine conversation.
- Memories that can be shared with family and community.

**What they should feel:**

- Welcome.
- Safe.
- Understood.
- Connected.
- Moved.
- Proud.
- Curious to return.

### Secondary audience: The Culturally Curious Explorer

International visitors from North America, Europe and elsewhere who want more
than a checklist of attractions. They may not be travelling for ancestral
reconnection, but they value local knowledge, food, history, creative culture,
nature and responsible participation.

### Growth audience: The Trusted Local Partner

International travel companies, educational institutions, corporate groups
and community organizations seeking an experienced Ghana-based operating
partner.

This audience requires a separate business-to-business message focused on
reliability, capacity, communication, risk management and customization. It
should not dominate the consumer homepage.

## 9. Customer jobs

Guests are not only hiring People & Places to arrange transport and tickets.
They are hiring the company to:

### Functional jobs

- Design a worthwhile itinerary.
- Handle transportation, timing, access and reservations.
- Provide a knowledgeable and dependable host.
- Adapt the experience to age, mobility, interests and budget.
- Explain what to expect before and during the visit.

### Emotional jobs

- Help them feel safe in an unfamiliar place.
- Give them permission to be curious and ask questions.
- Help them process emotionally significant history.
- Reduce the fear of missing the “real” Ghana.
- Make them feel cared for rather than processed.

### Social jobs

- Create stories they will be proud to share.
- Help families experience something meaningful together.
- Enable connection with heritage, community and contemporary Ghana.
- Give them confidence to recommend Ghana to others.

## 10. Brand promise

### Recommended promise

**We will introduce you to Ghana with the care, context and welcome we would
offer a friend.**

This promise is supported by the review evidence. Guests repeatedly describe
the team as warm, knowledgeable, flexible, present, organized, patient and
easy to trust.

### What the promise means operationally

- We listen before recommending an itinerary.
- We explain the story and significance behind a place.
- We communicate clearly before the guest arrives.
- We take punctuality, comfort and accessibility seriously.
- We allow room for reflection at emotionally significant sites.
- We adapt when plans or guest needs change.
- We remain present without making the experience feel controlled.
- We recommend places because they suit the guest, not because they are
  trending.

## 11. Brand pillars

### Pillar 1: Ghana, told from within

**Belief:** Ghana should be introduced by people who understand its everyday
life as well as its landmarks.

**Proof:**

- Ghanaian founders and hosts.
- Knowledge of local languages, communities and cultural context.
- Experience across hospitality, tourism, media and destination storytelling.
- Coverage of food, markets, creative life, family rhythms, history and
  landscapes.

**Expression:**

- Use named voices and first-hand observations.
- Explain why a place matters, not only what it is called.
- Show contemporary Ghana alongside history and heritage.
- Make regional and cultural distinctions rather than speaking about Ghana as
  one uniform experience.

### Pillar 2: Hospitality that feels personal

**Belief:** The quality of an experience is shaped by how a guest is welcomed
and cared for.

**Proof:**

- Reviews describe guests feeling like friends or family.
- Guests cite warmth, humour, patience and attentiveness.
- Reviewers mention care for solo travellers, older family members and
  accessibility needs.
- The founders maintain relationships beyond the tour itself.

**Expression:**

- Write as a thoughtful host, not a salesperson.
- Let guests meet the real people behind the company.
- Show small acts of care, not vague claims of exceptional service.
- Make contact invitations conversational and low-pressure.

### Pillar 3: Culture with context

**Belief:** A meaningful cultural experience requires explanation, respect and
space to feel—not just access to an attraction.

**Proof:**

- Reviews praise the historical context provided at Accra and heritage sites.
- Guests describe Cape Coast and Assin Manso experiences as educational,
  spiritual, healing and grounding.
- Guides are praised for understanding the emotional weight of heritage
  experiences.

**Expression:**

- Treat heritage sites with emotional intelligence.
- Avoid reducing history to dramatic sales copy.
- Credit historians, guides, artisans and communities.
- Prepare guests for the meaning of an experience before asking them to book.

### Pillar 4: Every detail handled with care

**Belief:** Good planning should create freedom for the guest to be present.

**Proof:**

- Reviews mention clear communication, timely pickups and smooth booking.
- Guests praise customization, flexibility and resourcefulness.
- Reviewers describe the experience as seamless and stress-free.
- The team has accommodated last-minute bookings and accessibility needs.

**Expression:**

- Be transparent about inclusions, pricing and response times.
- Explain the booking and planning process in plain language.
- Demonstrate preparedness rather than claiming perfection.
- Present premium quality through attention, consistency and restraint.

## 12. Brand personality

People & Places is:

- **Warm:** We make people feel welcome before asking them to commit.
- **Grounded:** We speak from experience and avoid inflated promises.
- **Curious:** We notice the stories, details and people others pass by.
- **Generous:** We share context and help guests feel informed.
- **Assured:** We are capable and professional without sounding corporate.
- **Emotionally intelligent:** We recognize that history, heritage and
  homecoming can carry personal weight.
- **Modern Ghanaian:** We show Ghana as living, creative and contemporary,
  without disconnecting it from history and tradition.

People & Places is not:

- A hype-driven travel plug.
- A faceless package reseller.
- A luxury brand built on exclusivity or status.
- A heritage brand that treats Ghana only as the past.
- A charity narrative about Africa.
- A collection of generic “bucket list” claims.
- A brand that performs Ghanaian identity through stereotypes.

### Brand archetypes

- **Primary: The Host** — welcoming, attentive and reassuring.
- **Secondary: The Storyteller** — observant, memorable and culturally
  grounded.
- **Supporting: The Guide** — knowledgeable, calm and capable.

## 13. Voice and writing principles

### 1. Speak from Ghana, not about Ghana from a distance

Prefer first-hand knowledge, names and sensory details.

**Prefer:** “Kojo takes guests through Makola early, while the market is still
settling into its rhythm.”

**Avoid:** “Explore the vibrant sights and sounds of magical Accra.”

### 2. Sound like a thoughtful host

Use natural, direct language. Invitations should feel personal, not urgent.

**Prefer:** “Tell us what is bringing you to Ghana.”

**Avoid:** “Book your unforgettable adventure today!”

### 3. Be specific enough to be believed

Replace superlatives with observable details.

**Prefer:** “Learn what the colours and patterns in kente communicate.”

**Avoid:** “Discover Ghana's most amazing hidden gems.”

### 4. Let emotion emerge from the story

Do not instruct people to feel transformed. Show the circumstances that made
an experience meaningful.

**Prefer:** “The family laid a wreath bearing their name, then took time to
reflect together.”

**Avoid:** “Prepare for a life-changing ancestral journey.”

### 5. Use confidence without absolutes

Avoid “the best,” “premier,” “perfect,” “always,” “every” and “guaranteed”
unless the statement can be proven and maintained.

### 6. Use Ghanaian language with purpose

Words such as *akwaaba* should appear when they add cultural meaning, not as
decoration. Explain unfamiliar terms naturally and respect regional language
differences.

### 7. Never manufacture humanity

- Do not invent guests, team members, quotations or biographies.
- Do not use stock portraits as employees or reviewers.
- Do not imply a photograph shows a People & Places experience when it does
  not.
- Do not present composite or rewritten testimonials as verbatim reviews.

## 18. Photography and film principles

### Visual purpose

Photography should not merely prove that a destination is beautiful. It should
show what happened between people.

### Priority subjects

- Guests and hosts in genuine conversation.
- Hands making food, cloth, pottery and art.
- Reactions, pauses, laughter and reflection.
- Everyday life around the experience.
- Guides sharing context rather than posing as authority figures.
- Families and multigenerational groups experiencing Ghana together.
- Contemporary Ghanaian creativity, hospitality and city life.
- Landscapes shown in relationship to human experience.

### Recommended mix

- 70% people and interaction.
- 20% environmental context.
- 10% iconic destination imagery.

### Standards

- Use real People & Places experiences wherever possible.
- Record names, locations, dates, permissions and story context.
- Obtain appropriate consent from guests and community participants.
- Credit cultural contributors.
- Do not use stock people as founders, staff, partners or customers.
- Do not reproduce a reviewer's image without permission.
- Avoid visual clichés that flatten Ghana into colour, poverty, wildlife or
  spectacle.

### Placeholder policy

- Clearly labelled neutral placeholders may be used during architecture,
  development and UI review.
- A placeholder must never imply that a stock person is a founder, host,
  partner, reviewer or guest.
- Critical public-launch slots—including the hero, founders, featured tours
  and guest story—must ultimately use accurate approved media.
- Missing originals do not block Sprint 0 or technical architecture. They
  should be uploaded and replaced through the future CMS before public launch.

### Review presentation

- Reviewer portraits are not required.
- Default to a clean text-led treatment with the reviewer name, star rating,
  review date or year, Google attribution and a link to the source.
- Keep the reviewer's wording verbatim. A shorter excerpt may be selected, but
  it must not be rewritten as though it were the original quotation.
- An approved tour photograph may be associated with a review when useful,
  but it should remain optional.

### CMS and media principle

The future site must provide an authenticated content-management path for
adding and updating tours, uploading photographs and video, selecting
featured content, maintaining review excerpts and publishing changes without
editing source code. Platform selection and the detailed data model belong to
the architecture sprint.

## 19. Editorial storytelling territories

Content should build recognition around a few repeatable territories.

### The people

Guides, artisans, cooks, historians, drivers, hosts, guests and community
members.

### The everyday

Family meals, markets opening, neighbourhood rhythms, Sunday gatherings,
street food, conversation and travel between destinations.

### The meaning

The history, symbolism and living context behind places, food, textiles,
rituals and traditions.

### The encounter

What surprised a guest, what changed their understanding and what they carried
home.

### The contemporary

Ghana's creative industries, music, art, hospitality, cities and modern
identity.

### Before you arrive

Practical and honest guidance that helps travellers arrive informed and
confident.

## 22. Homepage narrative brief

This is the strategic sequence for the future messaging sprint, not a layout
specification.

2. **Point of view:** Explain why the founders created People & Places.
3. **Human proof:** Introduce real hosts and a specific guest moment.
4. **Possibility:** Show several ways to experience culture, heritage, food,

## 24. Premium experience principles

Premium is not defined by repeatedly saying “luxury.” For People & Places,
premium means:

- The guest feels known.
- The experience has a clear point of view.
- Information arrives before it is needed.
- Hosts notice individual comfort and accessibility needs.
- Plans are dependable without feeling rigid.
- Cultural experiences are contextualized and respectful.
- Photography, language and materials are original and considered.
- Communication is calm, timely and consistent.
- The company makes fewer promises and keeps them well.
