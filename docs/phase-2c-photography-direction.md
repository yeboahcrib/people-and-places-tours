# Phase 2C — Photography Direction Guide

The official photography guide for People & Places. It covers both what the
photographs must feel like and, practically, how many of them are needed and in
what order.

**What this does not restate.** Brand Foundation §18 sets subject standards —
70% people and interaction, 20% environmental context, 10% iconic destination,
consent recorded, no stock person standing in for a founder, host or guest.
Phase 2A §6 sets treatment. Phase 2B §9 sets hierarchy. Those are settled.

---

## In one line

> **Photograph the welcome, not the destination.**

Everything below follows from that. A photograph of a beautiful place says
Ghana is beautiful, which nobody doubts. A photograph of somebody showing you
that place says something only this company can say.

---

## 1. Philosophy

Three tests. A photograph earns its place if it passes all three.

**Could only we have taken this?** If a stock library could supply it, it says
nothing about being hosted. This is the sharpest filter available and it
eliminates most travel photography.

**Is something happening between people?** Foundation §18 asks for 70% people
and interaction. The measure is not whether a person is present but whether
something is passing between two of them — attention, instruction, laughter,
quiet.

**Would the person in it be glad to see it published?** A test of respect, and
in practice also a test of whether the moment is real. People are rarely glad
to see themselves posed.

---

## 2. Emotional framework

The brief proposes The Call → Arrival → Discovery → Connection → Belonging →
Home, and asks whether it should evolve. It was already evaluated in 2A §2 and
that resolution stands, so rather than re-open it:

**Most visitors arrive mid-arc.** Nineteen indexable pages, Open Graph on all
of them, and a sharing pattern built on people sending links to family. A
sequence that only works when read in order will, for most people, never be
read.

**And it ends at "Home" for everyone**, which is true for a returner and an
overclaim for a first-time visitor. The site does not tell people what they
feel.

So photography serves **a three-beat loop that works on any single page**:

| Beat | What photography must do | The failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recognition** | Prove this was made by someone who knows. A named place, a real hour, a particular hand | A generic landscape |
| **Trust** | Show people who look competent and at ease. Steadiness, not glamour | A staged smile |
| **Invitation** | Leave room for the viewer. Someone looking into the frame, a path, an open door | A closed, complete tableau |

The **long arc** belongs to the homepage alone, and stops at **Belonging** — the
last word is the visitor's.

## 3. Categories

For each: what it must communicate, how to recognise a good one, and the way it
usually fails.

### Hero

**Communicates:** human presence at scale. Somebody, in a place, at a moment.

**Good one:** a person or small group reading as part of the landscape rather
than posed in front of it. Space to one side for a headline. Light that says a
time of day.

**Fails as:** an empty landmark. A drone shot with nobody in it. Anything that
would work equally well for any Ghanaian tour operator.

### Lifestyle — the backbone

The largest category and the most important. Conversations, walking together,
shared meals, learning something with your hands.

**Good one:** two or more people, one shared activity, nobody aware of the
camera. The host is participating, not presenting.

**Fails as:** a guide gesturing at a thing while guests watch politely. That is
a diagram of a tour, not a moment in one.

### Portrait

**Communicates:** this is a person you will actually meet.

**Good one:** in the environment where they work. Hands doing something. An
expression between expressions — the turn before the laugh, not the laugh.

**Fails as:** a corporate headshot. Plain background, folded arms, a smile held
too long. Foundation §18 already forbids stock people here; this forbids stock
*poses*.

**Required:** both founders, individually, working. The About page currently
shows two initials in circles.

### Heritage

Cape Coast, Elmina, Assin Manso. These carry the Silent register permanently
(2B §3), and that constrains the photography as much as the design.

- **No treatment.** No mask, no overlay, no filter, no environmental framing.
- **No golden-hour styling.** Making the Door of No Return look beautiful is a
  category error. Photograph it plainly.
- **Context over spectacle.** A guide explaining, a hand on a wall, the scale of
  a room. Not the dramatic wide shot.
- **Do not photograph strangers grieving.** Visitors weep at these sites. A
  camera turned on that is exploitative however good the result. If a guest
  wants their own moment recorded, that is their request to make, not ours to
  take.

### Food

**Communicates:** a meal is a social act here.

**Good one:** hands serving, a table mid-conversation, a pot being stirred, the
market stall it came from.

**Fails as:** a plated dish shot from above. That is restaurant marketing. Food
photographed alone tells you what you will eat; food photographed with people
tells you who you will eat it with.

### Nature

**Communicates:** exploration, not scenery.

**Good one:** somebody in it — walking, standing, looking. A human figure gives
scale and turns a view into an experience.

**Fails as:** a postcard. A waterfall with nobody near it is a screensaver.

Drone footage: **sparingly, and never as an opener.** It shows a place from a
position no guest will ever occupy, which is the opposite of the brand. Useful
occasionally for scale; never for atmosphere.

### Community

The most delicate category, and the distinction that matters most:

> **Participation, not observation.**

**Good one:** shot from *inside* the group — the photographer is part of what is
happening. Markets, music, craft, celebration, ordinary streets.

**Fails as:** photographing people as subjects of interest. A long lens across a
market is documentary in the worst sense: it makes people into scenery. If the
people in the frame do not know you are there, ask before you publish.

Consent matters most here. Foundation §18 requires it; this is the category
where it is most often skipped.

## 4. Composition

1. **Frame for the gaze.** Where the subject looks decides the crop. A person
   looking into the page pulls the reader with them; looking out of it pushes
   them away.
2. **Leave room for an unknown crop.** The same photograph may appear at
   600 × 400 and 1920 × 400. A tightly framed shot cannot become a letterbox.
   Shoot the full scene.
3. **Leave one quiet corner.** Hero images carry headlines. Sky, water, a wall,
   shade — one calm area makes an image usable where a busy one is not.
4. **Depth over flatness.** Something near, something far. Flat images read as
   stock.
5. **Off-centre.** The site's layouts are asymmetric; centred symmetrical
   photography fights them.
6. **Take the moment either side of the peak.** The turn before the laugh reads
   as real. The peak reads as posed.
7. **Hold up small.** Most people see these on a phone where a card is
   thumbnail-sized. One clear subject beats a rich scene.
8. **Let the light stand.** No heavy uniform grade. Ghanaian light is the
   strongest asset in the shoot and stylising it into a "look" is the first
   step toward cliché.

## 5. Diversity

Representation here is not a quota exercise. It is a question of whether people
can see themselves being welcomed.

Photography must include, over the whole set:

- **Diaspora visitors.** The Brand Foundation names the returner as the
  **primary** audience, and the site now leads with that in words. If the
  photography shows only white or non-Black visitors, the pictures contradict
  the copy — and pictures win.
- **A range of ages**, including older guests, who are well represented in the
  real reviews.
- **Families and multigenerational groups**, solo travellers, couples, and
  groups of friends.
- **Local communities as participants**, named where possible, never as
  anonymous background texture.
- **Both founders**, and hosts other than the founders.

**Authentic rather than staged.** Assembling a group to look diverse produces
photographs that look assembled. Photograph the guests who actually come; over a
season the range is real without being arranged.

---

## 6. Things we never photograph

- Stock people, in any role. Never a founder, host, guest, partner or reviewer.
- **AI-generated images.** Not for heroes, not for backgrounds, not for
  placeholders. A brand built on "these are the real people who live here"
  cannot use invented ones, even temporarily.
- Identifiable people without recorded permission.
- Guests grieving at heritage sites.
- Isolated plated dishes.
- Empty landmarks.
- Corporate headshots.
- Drone footage as an opener or as atmosphere.
- Long-lens photographs of people who do not know they are being photographed.
- Heavy grading, faux-vintage filters, or a uniform "look".
- Anything that flattens Ghana into colour, poverty, wildlife or spectacle.
- Photographs whose only interesting quality is the location. If anyone could
  have taken it, it says nothing about being hosted by us.

## 7. Environmental integration

How photography and the Environmental Design System share a page. 2B governs;
this is the photography side of the contract.

**Where environment disappears entirely:**
- Heritage imagery — no mask, no overlay, no framing
- The booking flow
- Any image where a face reaches the edge of the frame

**Which images need more space around them:**
- Anchors (full-bleed) — the environment recedes to tone only
- Portraits — never masked tighter than the shoulders
- Any image carrying text — the treatment is a legibility scrim, not a shape

**How environment supports rather than competes:**
- It **frames, masks or grounds**. It never overlays a subject.
- It respects the **hotspot** an editor sets in Sanity. The focal point is the
  part a mask may never cover.
- It loads **after** photography, always. Nothing decorative delays an image.

**The test**, from 2B: if your eye reaches the decoration before the
photograph, the decoration is wrong.

## 8. Asset collection checklist

Counted from the built site, not estimated. **87 photographic slots. 82 are
currently stock. 5 are ours.**

| Shape | Slots | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 600 × 400 landscape | 44 | Experience detail pages |
| 800 × 550 landscape | 15 | Experience cards |
| 1920 × 400 wide band | 14 | Experience page headers |
| 1920 × 720 wide hero | 5 | About, Contact, 404, Thanks |
| 675–1000 × 900 portrait | 6 | Homepage pathway cards |
| 1120 × 1400 tall | 1 | Homepage review band |
| 700 × 850 portrait | 1 | About founder figure |

**Landscape is the working shape** — 59 of 87. Shoot landscape by default.

### Priority, if the shoot runs short

**Tier 1**
1. Both founders, individually, working
2. A host and a guest in conversation — there is currently not one such
   photograph on the site, and it is the entire brand promise
3. Homepage hero

**Tier 2**
4. Six pathway images: History & Memory, Food & Everyday Life, Nature &
   Stillness, Adventure, Craft & Tradition, The Longer Story
5. One card image per experience — 15 needed
6. Cape Coast and Elmina, under §3 heritage rules

**Tier 3**
7. Detail shots — hands, food, a road, a doorway. These fill the 44 landscape
   slots and are gathered opportunistically.

### Capture this on the day

Trivial then, painful to reconstruct later. Every field already exists in
Sanity, and the site refuses to publish an image without a description.

- **Who** is in it, names spelled correctly
- **Where**, specifically — "Jamestown", not "Accra"
- **When** — date and roughly the hour, because light matters
- **Permission** — did identifiable people agree? A verbal yes in a note is
  enough on the day; nothing is enough later
- **Who took it**, for crediting
- **One sentence describing it** — this becomes what a blind visitor hears and
  what Google reads. Written on the day it is specific; written months later
  from a thumbnail it is generic

A shared note or spreadsheet is sufficient. It does not need to be in Sanity
yet.

## 9. Recommendations for asset curation

**Hero video.** The homepage currently plays a video hosted on a third-party
CDN nobody here controls — a dependency worth removing. A replacement should be
short, quiet, and loop without a visible seam; it should show people, not
sweeping scenery; and it must have a still poster frame that works alone, since
many visitors will only ever see that. Video is linked rather than uploaded, to
keep the site fast.

**Experience galleries.** Not yet built. When they are, they need 4–6 images
per experience with one clear Anchor. Do not build a gallery that needs twelve
photographs per tour; that is a commitment nobody will keep up.

**Team photography.** Both founders now, other hosts as the team grows. Reshoot
when someone's role changes rather than keeping a portrait that no longer looks
like them.

**Testimonial imagery.** Foundation §18 is explicit that reviewer portraits are
not required and the default is a clean text-led treatment. Do not photograph
guests to illustrate their reviews. If an approved tour photograph happens to
suit a review, it may accompany it.

**Contact and About imagery.** These pages should show the team and the place
they work, not a landscape. Somebody arriving at Contact wants to know who they
are writing to.

**Upload through Sanity, not the codebase.** Set the hotspot on every image —
that is what lets one photograph serve a card, a band and a hero without being
recomposed, and it is the region masks are forbidden from covering.

**Replace in priority order, not page by page.** A site with real founders and
stock landscapes is better than one with real landscapes and initials where the
founders should be.

---

## 10. Success criteria

The photography succeeds when a returning visitor recognises the people in it
as the ones who will meet them, and a first-time visitor sees a country rather
than a destination.

The direction succeeds when, in a year, nobody has to ask where a photograph
was taken, who is in it, or whether we had permission.

The set succeeds when it reads as one family — not because it was graded to
match, but because the same person was interested in the same things.

And all of it succeeds if the site says, without writing it down:

> **You are not just visiting Ghana. You are being welcomed into it.**
