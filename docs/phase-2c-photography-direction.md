# Phase 2C — Photography Direction

A brief for the shoot, and the rules for what happens to the images afterwards.

**What this document is not.** The Brand Foundation §18 already sets the
subject standards — 70% people and interaction, 20% environmental context, 10%
iconic destination, consent recorded, no stock person standing in for a founder
or guest. Phase 2A §6 sets the treatment. Phase 2B §9 sets the hierarchy. Those
are settled and are not restated here.

What was missing is the practical part: **how many photographs, of what, in
what shape, in what order** — and what has to be captured on the day so the
images are usable afterwards.

---

## 1. What the site actually needs

Counted from the built site, not estimated.

**87 photographic slots. 82 are stock. 5 are ours.**

| Shape | Slots | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **600 × 400** landscape | 44 | Experience detail pages — the workhorse |
| **800 × 550** landscape | 15 | Experience cards on the listing page |
| **1920 × 400** wide band | 14 | Experience detail page headers |
| **1920 × 720** wide hero | 5 | Page heroes: About, Contact, 404, Thanks |
| **675–1000 × 900** portrait-ish | 6 | Homepage pathway cards |
| **1120 × 1400** tall portrait | 1 | Homepage review band |
| **700 × 850** portrait | 1 | About page founder figure |

Two things follow:

**Landscape is the working shape.** 59 of 87 slots are 3:2 or thereabouts.
Shoot landscape by default and crop later.

**Shoot wider than you think.** The 1920-wide bands are only 400px tall — an
extreme letterbox. A photograph composed for that crop is unusable anywhere
else, so shoot the full scene and let the crop happen in Sanity, where the
hotspot decides what survives.

---

## 2. Priority order

If the shoot runs short, this is the order that matters. It follows visibility
and irreplaceability, not convenience.

**Tier 1 — nothing else matters as much**

1. **The two founders.** Individually, working, not posed against a wall. The
   About page currently shows initials in circles because no real portrait
   exists. This is the single most conspicuous gap on the site.
2. **Hosts with guests, mid-conversation.** The brand promise is being welcomed
   by people who live there. There is currently not one photograph of that
   happening.
3. **Homepage hero.** One image, landscape, wide enough to survive a very wide
   crop. It carries the first impression.

**Tier 2 — the pages people actually reach**

4. **The six pathway images** (History & Memory, Food & Everyday Life, Nature &
   Stillness, Adventure, Craft & Tradition, The Longer Story). Portrait
   orientation, one strong image each.
5. **One card image per experience** — 15 needed, 800 × 550.
6. **Cape Coast and Elmina.** See §5; different rules apply.

**Tier 3 — depth**

7. Detail shots for the experience pages: hands working, food being made, a
   road, a doorway. These fill the 44 landscape slots and are the easiest to
   gather opportunistically.

---

## 3. What makes a photograph usable here

Beyond Foundation §18, three practical constraints that come from the site
itself:

**It has to survive an unknown crop.** The same photograph may appear as a
600 × 400 card and a 1920 × 400 band. Leave room around the subject. A tightly
framed portrait cannot become a letterbox.

**It has to work under text.** Hero images carry a headline. Photographs with a
calm area — sky, water, wall, shade — are far more useful than ones busy edge
to edge. This is not a request for empty photographs, only for one quiet corner.

**It has to hold up small.** Most people will see these on a phone, where a
600 × 400 card is thumbnail-sized. Images that depend on fine detail read as
noise. One clear subject beats a rich scene.

---

## 4. Capture this on the day

This is the part that is painful to reconstruct later and trivial at the time.
Sanity has a field for each of these, and the site refuses to publish an image
without a description.

For every photograph worth keeping:

- **Who is in it** — names, spelled correctly
- **Where** — the place, specifically. "Jamestown" not "Accra"
- **When** — date, and roughly the hour, because light matters
- **Permission** — did identifiable people agree to it being published? A
  verbal yes recorded in a note is enough; nothing is enough later
- **Who took it** — for crediting
- **One sentence describing it** — this becomes the description a blind visitor
  hears and Google reads. Written on the day by someone who was there, it is
  specific. Written months later from a thumbnail, it is generic

A shared note or spreadsheet on the day is sufficient. It does not need to be
in Sanity yet.

---

## 5. Heritage photography — different rules

Cape Coast, Elmina and Assin Manso carry the Silent register permanently
(2B §3). That constrains the photography as well as the design:

- **No treatment.** These images take no mask, no overlay, no filter, no
  environmental framing. They appear as they were taken.
- **Photograph the place, not people grieving in it.** Visitors weep at these
  sites. A camera turned on a stranger's grief is exploitative, however
  beautiful the result. If a guest wants their own moment recorded, that is
  their request to make, not ours to take.
- **No golden-hour styling.** Making the Door of No Return look beautiful is a
  category error. Photograph it plainly.
- **Context over spectacle.** A guide explaining, a hand on a wall, the scale of
  a room. Not the dramatic wide shot.

---

## 6. What we will not use

- Stock photography of people, anywhere, in any role.
- Any identifiable person without recorded permission.
- Images of guests at heritage sites showing distress.
- Heavy uniform grading. Ghanaian light is the strongest asset in the shoot;
  stylising it into a "look" is the first step toward the cliché the Brand
  Foundation warns against.
- Photographs where the only interesting thing is the location. If it could
  have been taken by anyone, it says nothing about being hosted by us.
- Anything that flattens Ghana into colour, poverty, wildlife or spectacle.

---

## 7. After the shoot

**Upload through Sanity, not into the codebase.** The media model is built:
hotspot cropping, required descriptions, and an approval gate that prevents an
unapproved image reaching the live site.

**Set the hotspot on every image.** Drag the circle over what must stay visible.
This is what lets one photograph serve a card, a band and a hero without being
recomposed each time, and environmental masks are forbidden from covering it
(2B §6).

**Replace in priority order** (§2), not page by page. A site with real founders
and stock landscapes is far better than one with real landscapes and initials
where the founders should be.

**Only then does environmental design begin.** 2B §10 holds: a section's
environment is not finalised until its real photograph exists, because a mask
tuned to a placeholder breaks when the real image lands.

---

## 8. What this unblocks

Replacing the photography is the largest single improvement available to this
site, and it is not close. The About page promises *"we would rather show you
real ones than borrowed ones."* Until the shoot, that sentence sits above two
sets of initials and 82 stock images.

Once the images land, in order:

1. Photography integration through Sanity, with a treatment pass — crops,
   captions, and the legibility scrim where text sits over an image
2. Fluid display type, the last of the type system
3. Environmental design, starting with the Quiet and Silent registers

---

## Success criteria

The shoot succeeded if a returning visitor recognises the people in the
photographs as the ones who will meet them, and if a first-time visitor sees a
country rather than a destination.

The direction succeeded if, in a year, nobody has to ask where a photograph was
taken, who is in it, or whether we had permission to use it.
