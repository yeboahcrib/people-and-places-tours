# People & Places Homepage Messaging Brief

**Version:** 1.7 draft  
**Prepared:** July 2026  
**Owner:** Brand and content architecture  
**Status:** Founder review required before technical architecture or UI work  
**Depends on:** `docs/people-and-places-brand-foundation.md`

## 1. Purpose

This brief defines the story the People & Places homepage must tell, the job
of each section, the proof required, the calls to action and the media needed
to support the message.

It is not:

- A visual design.
- A component specification.
- Final production copy.
- Permission to change the current website.

The content architecture should be approved before Codex defines the technical
architecture and before Claude Code begins UI or usability work.

## 2. Main goal

The homepage must establish People & Places as a trusted, premium,
story-led Ghana destination brand rather than a generic tour agency.

The visitor should feel inspired before being asked to compare tours or make a
booking decision.

## 3. Audience

### Primary

Black Americans and members of the African diaspora considering a first visit,
a return, a heritage-focused visit, a family experience or a milestone trip
to Ghana.

### Secondary

International travellers seeking culturally grounded, locally hosted
experiences of Ghana.

### Homepage inclusion principle

The homepage should acknowledge that Ghana may feel like a return for one
guest and a first meeting for another. Neither group should feel like an
afterthought.

Business-to-business partners, corporate groups and educational groups remain
important but should use a secondary pathway rather than shaping the main
homepage message.

## 4. Five-second communication test

Within five seconds, a new visitor should understand:

1. **Where:** People & Places helps people experience Ghana.
2. **What:** The company provides thoughtfully hosted cultural and travel
   experiences.
3. **Who:** The tours are hosted by Ghanaians who know the country from
   within.
4. **Difference:** Guests receive context, personal care and human connection,
   not only an itinerary.
5. **Next step:** They can explore tours or begin a conversation.

### Intended first emotion

**Recognition and curiosity:** “This feels like a thoughtful way to meet
Ghana.”

### Intended trust response

**Calm confidence:** “These are real people, with real guests and evidence of
care.”

## 5. Core message

### Public brand presentation

**People & Places**

No permanent descriptor is currently proposed. The rejected
“Ghanaian-hosted journeys” line should not be replaced with another generic
category label merely to fill the space.

### Homepage headline status

**Provisionally approved:** Your time in Ghana, hosted by people born and
raised here.

### Supporting message status

**Provisionally approved:** We share Ghana's history, food, culture and
everyday life as we know them firsthand, shaping your time here around what
brings you.

These lines may be used to continue Sprint 0 but must be revisited during the
final messaging review before implementation. The earlier headline “Meet
Ghana through the people who call it home” remains rejected.

Do not automatically substitute “experiences,” which risks replacing one
generic travel-category word with another.

### Primary call to action

**See Ghana with us**

This leads to the experience pathways or featured tours—not immediately to a
generic booking form.

### Secondary call to action

**Tell us what brings you here**

This begins a conversation through the planning/contact path.

### Why the supporting message works

- It explains that People & Places shapes the visitor's time around what
  matters to them.
- It grounds the brand's point of view in firsthand knowledge.
- It leaves space for heritage, contemporary life and different reasons for
  travelling.
- It avoids the overused “authentic,” “hidden gems” and “unforgettable”
  language of the travel category.
- It centers the visitor without making an inflated emotional promise.

The headline names Ghana immediately, allowing “here” in the supporting
message to remain clear.

## 6. Narrative strategy

### Competitive stress test

Current Ghana travel competitors commonly claim authenticity, immersion,
transformation, local expertise, seamless planning, heritage connection and
premium care. Those ideas are category expectations rather than distinctive
positions.

The strongest competitors also provide operational proof:

- [Ashanti African Tours](https://ashantiafricantours.com/) foregrounds
  credentials, specialist expertise, policies and sustainability.
- [Travel Time Africa](https://traveltimeafrica.com/) combines premium
  service, visible pricing, reviews and practical trust infrastructure.
- [Cultured Ghana](https://culturedghana.com/) occupies culture, community,
  return and luxe-experience territory.
- [Protour Africa](https://protour.africa/) focuses tightly on diaspora
  homecoming and ancestral reconnection.
- [African Roots Travel](https://www.africanrootstravel.com/) emphasizes
  Ghana-based presence, family feeling and living culture.
- [Nkabom Culture](https://www.nkabomculture.com/) specializes in diaspora,
  institutional and HBCU-oriented experiences.
- [Sankofa Journeys](https://www.sankofabra.com/) uses return, roots and
  spiritual-pilgrimage language.

These are competitor-published claims, not independently certified facts.

People & Places should not try to sound more authentic, luxurious or
transformative than these brands. Its defensible whitespace is:

- Ghana introduced through actual human encounters.
- Contemporary and everyday Ghana held alongside history and heritage.
- Hosting demonstrated as the product through specific acts of attention.
- Emotion allowed to emerge from real guest stories rather than promised in
  advance.
- The founders' original “I never knew Ghana looked like this” insight.
- Quietly premium care expressed through pace, preparation, context and a host
  who notices.

### Narrative flow

The homepage should move through five emotional phases:

| Phase | Visitor movement | Desired emotion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invitation | From unfamiliarity to interest | Curiosity |
| Recognition | From interest to understanding the founders' point of view | Connection |
| Possibility | From understanding to imagining their time in Ghana | Desire |
| Proof and care | From desire to confidence | Trust |
| Conversation | From confidence to an easy next step | Welcome |

The page should not behave like:

> Hero → package grid → claims → booking instructions

The preferred story is:

> Invitation → founder story → ways to experience Ghana → available tours →
> how guests are hosted → guest story → reviews and trust → planning process
> → Instagram and stories → final invitation

### Visitor question

“What is this, and is it relevant to the Ghana experience I want?”

### Message job

- Name Ghana immediately.
- Introduce the locally hosted difference.
- Balance heritage and homecoming with contemporary discovery.
- Create emotional interest without withholding basic clarity.

### Recommended copy direction

**Descriptor:** None currently proposed. Do not force a slogan-like category
label.

**Working headline:** Your time in Ghana, hosted by people born and raised
here.

**Working supporting copy:** We share Ghana's history, food, culture and
everyday life as we know them firsthand, shaping your time here around what
brings you.

**Primary CTA:** See Ghana with us

**Secondary CTA:** Tell us what brings you here

**Optional compact proof line:** Founded in Ghana in 2021 · 5.0 on Google
from 15 reviews as of July 2026

The proof line must link to the Google Business Profile and be updated when
the review count or average changes.

### Media requirement

Use owned People & Places film or photography showing a guest-host encounter,
not a generic aerial destination montage. The media should contain:

- A recognizable Ghanaian context.
- People in interaction.
- One quiet or reflective moment.
- One expression of joy or welcome.
- Contemporary life as well as place.

### Avoid

- The current externally hosted hero video unless ownership and usage rights
  are confirmed.
- A headline that could describe any country.
- “The ultimate Ghana adventure.”
- Booking pressure.
- Text embedded inside the image or video.

## 9. Section 2 — Founder story

### Visitor question

“Why did these people create this company?”

### Message job

- Introduce Isaac and Kojo as real founders.
- Turn the brand mission into a human observation.
- Explain the gap between Ghana as lived and Ghana as perceived.
- Establish visual storytelling as part of the brand's origin.

### Recommended copy direction

**Eyebrow:** Why People & Places exists

**Headline:** “I never knew Ghana looked like this.”

**Body direction:**

People kept sending Isaac and Kojo that message whenever they shared
photographs from home. Kojo heard the same surprise while hosting
international visitors. Ghana's landscapes, modern life, food, creative
energy and everyday warmth did not match the narrow story many guests had
inherited.

They founded People & Places in 2021 to tell a fuller story—first through
photography and film, and then through tours and visits that let people
experience Ghana for themselves.

**CTA:** Meet Isaac and Kojo

### Media requirement

- Use the founder-approved photograph showing Isaac in yellow and Evans in
  white, subject to having a high-resolution original.
- Include names and actual roles.
- A short founder-film excerpt may eventually provide stronger proof than a
  static biography.

### Host-profile requirement

The homepage treatment should make both founders visible by name rather than
leaving them behind an About-page link:

- **Evans Yirenkyi (Kojo):** Co-founder and Manager; cultural interpretation,
  operations and guest care.
- **Isaac Yeboah (Nana Yeboah):** Co-founder and Tech Lead; visual
  storytelling, diaspora connection and experience design.

Keep the profiles short on the homepage and link to the full founding story.

### Avoid

- A stock “founder” photograph.
- Invented employees or titles.
- Claims that the founders are certified guides unless documented.
- A long corporate history.

## 10. Section 3 — Ways to experience Ghana

### Visitor question

“Which part of Ghana's story is calling me?”

### Message job

Broaden desire before presenting products. Show that Ghana can be encountered
through memory, food, craft, city life, nature, celebration and everyday
connection.

### Recommended copy direction

**Eyebrow:** Ways into Ghana

**Headline:** There is more than one way to meet Ghana.

**Introduction:** Some visits begin with history. Others begin with a plate
shared at the table, a pattern taking shape on a loom, music after dark or the
road opening beyond Accra.

### Recommended pathways

#### Heritage and homecoming

Walk through history with context, care and room to reflect.

#### Food and city life

Meet Accra through the places it eats, gathers, creates and stays awake.

#### Craft and tradition

Learn with the people who carry knowledge through cloth, clay, colour and
symbol.

#### Nature and adventure

Move from coast to forest, river and waterfall with people who know the way.

#### Celebrations and personal trips

Shape a birthday, family return, group tour or milestone around what matters
to you.

### CTA

Each pathway should lead to relevant tours or the custom-planning path. The
section-level CTA is:

**Find your way into Ghana**

### Media requirement

Each pathway requires a real moment involving people. Destination-only stock
photography does not meet the message requirement.

## 11. Section 4 — Available tours

### Visitor question

“What can I actually experience?”

### Message job

- Translate inspiration into clear options.
- Present a small, intentional selection rather than the entire inventory.
- Show emotional and practical relevance.

### Recommended copy direction

**Eyebrow:** Tours

**Headline:** Choose a place to begin.

**Introduction:** Book a day tour for any day with advance notice, explore a
tailored multi-day tour such as Just Go Ghana, or ask us to plan something
around your interests, dates and requests.

### Offer structure

| Offer type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Day tours | Existing day tours that customers can book for any day with advance notice; there are no guaranteed fixed daily departures |
| Tailored tours | Established multi-day offers, including Just Go Ghana, that provide a prepared starting point |
| Custom tours | Tours planned around the customer's dates, interests, group and requests |

The complete day-tour catalogue should have its own browse path. Custom tours
should have a direct conversation path rather than being forced into a
standard product card.

### Featured-tour approach

The founder has approved a small featured-tour selection on the homepage. It
is not intended to represent the full day-tour catalogue.

The exact set should be confirmed against commercial priority, operational
readiness, the complete tour inventory and available original media. The
homepage should initially feature three to five tours representing different
motivations:

- An ancestral or heritage tour.
- A contemporary Accra food or city tour.
- A craft or hands-on cultural tour.
- A nature or adventure tour.
- A tailored multi-day Ghana tour.

### Card message requirements

Each featured tour preview should answer:

- What human or cultural encounter defines it?
- Who is it best suited to?
- How long does it take?
- Where does it begin?
- What practical starting price or planning expectation is accurate?

### CTAs

- **View this tour**
- **See all day tours**
- **Explore Just Go Ghana**
- **Plan a custom tour**

Use **Check availability** only when the visitor reaches a specific tour.

### Avoid

- “Best seller,” “epic,” “thrill,” “ultimate” and similar promotional badges
  unless they communicate useful, verifiable information.
- Showing star icons on every tour without tour-specific review evidence.
- Leading every card with price before meaning.

## 12. Section 5 — How you are hosted

### Visitor question

“Why should I experience Ghana with People & Places?”

### Message job

Explain the brand's value through behaviours guests already recognize in
reviews, not through generic benefit claims.

### Recommended copy direction

**Eyebrow:** How we host

**Headline:** The difference is how you are welcomed.

**Introduction:** We listen before we plan, share the context behind each
place and take care of the details that allow you to be present.

**Working benefit statement:** Cultural context and personal care give you the
freedom to be present.

### Four proof-led principles

#### Context before checklists

You will understand why a place matters, not only that it was on the route.

#### Care you can feel

Pace, comfort, accessibility and personal needs are part of the plan.

#### Time shaped around you

We start with your reason for coming and adapt the experience around your
interests, time and group.

#### Ghana beyond the obvious

History matters, and so do contemporary food, creativity, family life, nature
and the everyday rhythm of the country.

### Founder voice

If a founder quotation is used, it must be real and approved. Recommended
source direction from Isaac's discovery response:

> It should feel less like a scheduled tour and more like catching up with an
> old friend.

Confirm the exact final wording with Isaac before presenting it as a direct
quotation.

### Avoid

- Expert Local Guides.
- Stress-Free Logistics.
- Small Group Vibes.
- Photo-Ready Moments.
- “Every route is personal. Every trip leaves a mark.”

These current messages are either generic, unverified across all services or
too promotional for the intended brand.

- At least six reference flexibility or personalization.

## 15. Section 8 — Planning process

### Visitor question

“What happens if I contact you?”

### Message job

Reduce planning anxiety without making the process sound cheap, instant or
automated.

### Recommended copy direction

**Eyebrow:** Planning your time in Ghana

**Headline:** From first hello to your arrival.

**Introduction:** Start with the reason you are coming. We will help shape the
route, pace and details around it.

### Three-step message

#### 1. Tell us what brings you to Ghana

Share your dates, group and what you hope to experience. The team usually
responds within one hour during Monday-Friday business hours.

#### 2. Shape your time in Ghana together

We recommend experiences, discuss pace and practical needs, and confirm what
is included before you commit.

#### 3. Arrive and be welcomed

Your host meets you at the agreed place and takes care of the confirmed
details so you can be present for your time here.

### CTAs

- **Plan your time in Ghana**
- **Talk with us on WhatsApp**

### Claims to defer

- A fixed deposit percentage until payment terms are documented.
- “Book in minutes.”
- Same-day confirmation for every service.
- 24/7 support.
- Guaranteed safety.
- Insurance or active tourism-licence claims.

**See Ghana with us**

Use in the hero and link to experience discovery.

### Personal conversation CTA

**Tell us what brings you here**

Use when the surrounding content emphasizes meaning, personalization or

### Supporting CTAs

- Meet Isaac and Kojo
- Find your way into Ghana
- View this tour
- See all day tours
- Read our Google reviews
- Follow @peopleand.places
- Talk with us on WhatsApp

**Check availability**

Reserve for a specific experience or date.

### CTA to de-emphasize

**Book now**

Use only at the final transactional step, not as the brand's dominant
invitation.

## 19. Voice guardrails for homepage copy

### The homepage should sound

- Ghanaian in point of view.
- Warm and conversational.
- Specific and observant.
- Calmly confident.
- Respectful of emotional complexity.
- Premium through restraint and care.

### The homepage should not sound

- Like a tourism board.
- Like a package marketplace.
- Like an influencer caption.
- Like an automated itinerary generator.
- Like a corporate mission statement.
- Like every visitor must experience Ghana in the same way.

### Words and patterns to reduce

- Authentic.
- Unforgettable.
- Hidden gems.
- Ultimate.
- Best.
- Premier.
- Breathtaking.
- Vibrant, when no specific detail follows.
- “Every guide. Every route. Every trip.”
- “Real tours. Real people. Real Ghana.”
- Exclamation marks as a substitute for warmth.

### Words and ideas to earn through evidence

- Welcome.
- Context.
- Care.
- Belonging.
- Perspective.
- Connection.
- Personal.
- Thoughtful.
- Ghanaian-hosted.

## 22. Content architecture requirements

Codex should define the technical content model after this brief is approved.
At minimum, the architecture must support:

- Global brand name and descriptor.
- Hero message, CTAs and owned media.
- Combined origin-and-hosts storytelling with real founder profiles.
- Experience pathways independent of individual tour inventory.
- Small featured-tour selection from the complete tour catalogue.
- Hosting principles with proof.
- A featured guest story.
- Structured, sourced testimonials.
- Maintainable trust facts with dates and links.
- Planning steps and response expectations.
- Instagram/editorial references.
- Final conversion message.

The site must also provide an authenticated CMS or comparable backend
interface through which authorized People & Places editors can:

- Create, edit, archive and feature tours.
- Distinguish day tours, tailored multi-day tours and custom-tour pathways.
- Upload and replace photographs and video.
- Store alt text, captions, ownership, permission and focal-point metadata.
- Select homepage media without changing source code.
- Add or update curated Google review excerpts and their source details.
- Manage draft and published states.
- Update trust facts and their verification dates.

The architecture sprint will select the platform and define roles, content
schemas, API boundaries, media handling and migration from the current static
catalogue. No CMS is selected by this Sprint 0 document.

Review content should support:

- Full review text exactly as sourced.
- Selected verbatim excerpt.
- Reviewer name.
- Star rating.
- Source.
- Source URL.
- Review date.
- Related tour or visit.
- Optional approved tour image.
- Image permission status when an image is present.
- Excerpt-versus-full-review status.
- Last source-verification date.
- Internal booking-match status, if the business later chooses to describe a
  review as a verified customer review.

Trust facts should support:

- Value.
- Label.
- Source.
- Verification date.
- Public display status.
