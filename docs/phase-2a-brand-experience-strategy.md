# Phase 2A — Brand Experience Strategy

The creative source of truth for Phase 2. Every background, illustration,
motion decision, photographic treatment and layout choice should be defensible
against this document.

**This builds on the Brand Foundation; it does not replace it.** Positioning
(§7), audience (§8), promise (§10), pillars (§11), voice (§13), photography
standards (§18) and editorial territories (§19) are already settled there and
are treated here as given. What the Foundation does not yet define is how any
of that becomes *environment*: light, depth, movement, silence, and the shape
of a page. That is this document's job.

---

## 0. Two conflicts to settle before designing anything

Design briefs fail quietly when they contradict an earlier approved document
and nobody notices. Two contradictions exist right now.

### 0.1 The audience question

The Phase 2A brief says the two audiences are equal: *"Neither audience should
feel secondary."* The approved Brand Foundation §8 says the opposite: the
diaspora traveller is the **primary** audience, and *"the homepage will lead
with the African diaspora."*

**These reconcile, but only in one direction, and the asymmetry is the whole
insight.**

Emotional registers are not symmetrical in who they exclude:

- Language written for the returner — arrival, welcome, recognition, being
  known — reads as warm hospitality to a first-time visitor. Nothing is lost
  on them.
- Language written for the discoverer — exotic, undiscovered, hidden gem,
  bucket list — actively alienates the returner. It tells someone returning to
  their ancestral home that they are a tourist in it.

So the resolution is: **lead with the emotional register of the returner,
because it is the one that includes everybody.** The brief's "neither should
feel secondary" is satisfied not by splitting the difference but by choosing
the register that has room for both.

Practically: the site never says "discover" where it can say "meet." It never
implies Ghana is new; it implies *you* are welcome.

### 0.2 The site does not currently do this

Measured across the codebase: the word "diaspora" appears **five times in two
files**, and "homecoming" twice in one — all of them inside FAQ answers.

The audience the Brand Foundation calls primary is currently addressed in the
least-read component on the site. Phase 1 built the product correctly; it did
not deliver the Foundation's audience strategy. Phase 2 must, and this is the
single most consequential thing it will do.

## 1. Brand Experience Vision

> **The website should feel like the hour before a journey begins, in the
> company of someone who already lives there.**

Not the brochure. Not the airport. The kitchen table the night before — maps
out, someone telling you which road is worth the extra hour and why, and the
quiet confidence of a person who has nothing to prove about their own home.

Three qualities follow from that image and govern everything:

**Composure.** A host who is anxious to impress is not restful. The site
should never look like it is trying hard. Restraint is hospitality.

**Specificity.** Friends do not speak in superlatives; they tell you the
particular thing. "The best of Ghana" is marketing. "The road to Aburi at
seven in the morning" is a friend.

**Room.** A welcome that fills every silence is not a welcome, it is a
performance. Negative space is not an absence of design here. It is the
brand's most characteristic gesture.

## 2. Emotional Narrative

### 2.1 Why the proposed arc does not survive contact with the site

The brief proposes: **The Call → The Journey → Discovery → Connection →
Belonging → Home**, and invites evaluation. It should not be adopted as given,
for two reasons.

**It assumes a front door.** The site has nineteen indexable URLs, Open Graph
on every one, and its sharing pattern is diaspora travellers sending links to
family. Most visitors will arrive mid-arc — on Cape Coast, on Contact, on a
shared experience page. An arc that only works when read in order will, for
most people, never be read at all.

**It ends at "Home" for everyone.** For the returner that is true and moving.
For a first-time Ghanaian-curious visitor from Manchester, telling them they
have come home is an overclaim, and overclaiming is precisely how warmth
becomes sentimentality. The Foundation's discipline about unverifiable claims
should extend to emotional ones.

### 2.2 What to use instead

**A repeatable three-beat loop on every page, and the long arc reserved for
the homepage alone.**

Every page, whatever its subject, should move the visitor through:

1. **Recognition** — *this was made by someone who knows.* Earned by
   specificity: a named place, a real hour of the day, a particular hand doing
   a particular thing.
2. **Trust** — *these people are steady.* Earned by clarity and restraint:
   plain answers, visible next steps, nothing hidden.
3. **Invitation** — *there is room for me here.* Earned by tone: an open door,
   never a countdown timer.

This loop is scale-independent. It works on a 400-word tour page and on the
homepage. It means a visitor who lands anywhere still receives the complete
brand in one screen.

The **long arc** — Call, Journey, Discovery, Connection, Belonging — belongs
to the homepage's seven existing sections, which already approximate it. It
should be tightened toward the arc, not rebuilt. And it should stop one step
short: end at **Belonging**, and let *Home* be something the visitor concludes
rather than something the site asserts.

> The brand's most powerful move is to leave the last word to the reader.

## 3. Visual Philosophy

The brief names Apple's restraint, Airbnb's warmth, editorial storytelling and
contemporary Ghanaian design. Those are four different centres of gravity, and
a blend of four things is usually a blur. The hierarchy that keeps it coherent:

**Structure from editorial. Temperature from hospitality. Discipline from
restraint. Authority from place.**

- **Editorial gives the bones.** Column, margin, caption, rhythm, the
  confidence to let one image run wide and the next sit small. Layout carries
  the storytelling; ornament does not.
- **Hospitality gives the warmth**, and it comes from *photography and
  language*, not from decoration. Airbnb's warmth is people, not flourishes.
- **Restraint is the editor.** For every element, the question is not "is this
  attractive" but "does its absence hurt." Most decoration fails that test.
- **Ghana gives the authority**, expressed through what is photographed and
  who is speaking — not through applied pattern.

### The governing rule

**Photography is the hero. Everything else is stagecraft, and stagecraft that
draws attention to itself has failed.**

A useful test: if a decorative element were removed and no one noticed, it was
noise. If removing it makes the page feel colder or less legible, it was
doing work. Only keep the second kind.

## 4. Environmental Storytelling Principles

Backgrounds should behave like **weather and time of day** — felt, rarely
looked at, never narrated.

### The density gradient

Environmental treatment is not distributed evenly. It follows emotional
temperature, and it is *inverse to gravity*: the heavier the content, the
quieter the environment.

| Register | Environmental density | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Open** | Fullest expression: horizon, layered depth, light | Homepage hero, final invitation |
| **Ambient** | Present but recessive: soft gradient, faint depth | Experiences listing, About story |
| **Quiet** | Near-absent: flat ground, generous space | Experience detail body, FAQs |
| **Silent** | None, deliberately | **Heritage content. The booking flow. Confirmation.** | 

### Two places decoration is forbidden, and why

**Sites of memory.** Cape Coast, Elmina, Assin Manso. A visitor may be
standing at the point where an ancestor was taken. Any decorative gesture near
that content — a wave, a flourish, a parallax — converts grief into styling.
The design response to that material is *space and stillness*, nothing else.
This is the clearest line in this document.

**The booking flow.** It is the moment a person decides to trust you with a
journey. Decoration reads as distraction exactly when attention matters most.
It stays as it is: calm, plain, uncluttered.

### Principles

1. **Horizon before ornament.** The Atlantic edge — a long, low, level line —
   is the single most useful environmental idea available. It is Ghana's
   southern boundary, the direction of departure and return, and formally it
   is just a horizontal. It carries meaning without illustrating anything.
2. **Depth, not pattern.** Layers that imply distance (near, middle, far) age
   better and stereotype less than any surface motif.
3. **Light is the warmest instrument available.** A gradient suggesting late
   afternoon does more emotional work than any shape, and cannot be a cliché.
4. **Backgrounds never cross the text.** If it reduces legibility by any
   measurable amount, it is wrong regardless of beauty.
5. **Sections must be allowed to be plain.** A page where every band is
   treated has no emphasis left to give.

## 5. Motion Philosophy

**Motion should behave like breath, not like animation.**

The tokens already exist from the Phase 1 consolidation and should be used
rather than extended: `--motion-instant` 120ms, `--motion-fast` 200ms,
`--motion-base` 300ms, `--motion-slow` 560ms, `--motion-reveal` 620ms, with
`--ease-out` `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` as the house curve.

That curve — fast to start, long to settle — is the brand in a single
function: arrives without hesitation, comes to rest without a bounce. It
should remain the default for essentially everything.

### Principles

1. **Motion clarifies; it does not perform.** Every movement should answer
   "what just changed" or "where am I."
2. **Nothing loops in the periphery.** Ambient perpetual motion is a
   television left on in the corner. Environmental movement, if any, is
   scroll-linked — the visitor drives it, and it stops when they stop.
3. **Slower for weight.** Heavier content resolves more slowly. Heritage
   content does not animate at all.
4. **Reduced motion is not a downgrade path, it is a second correct design.**
   Every environmental effect must be conceived with its still version at the
   same time.

### An open defect

The homepage testimonials carousel advances on `requestAnimationFrame` and is
the only animated element on the site that never checks
`prefers-reduced-motion`. It must be fixed before Phase 2 adds any motion, or
the new work inherits a broken foundation.

## 6. Photography Philosophy

Foundation §18 already sets the standard: 70% people and interaction, 20%
environmental context, 10% iconic destination, consent and credit required,
and no stock person may stand in for a founder, host, partner or guest. That
is correct and this document adds no new subject rules.

What Phase 2 must add is **treatment** — how photographs are held.

1. **Frame for the gaze, not the geography.** Where the subject's attention
   goes decides the crop. A person looking into the page pulls the reader
   with them.
2. **Prefer the moment either side of the peak.** The turn before the laugh
   reads as real; the peak reads as stock.
3. **Full-bleed is a punctuation mark, not a default.** If every image is
   enormous, none is.
4. **Let the natural light stand.** No heavy uniform grade. Ghanaian light is
   already the warmest asset available, and stylising it into a "look" is a
   step toward the cliché the Foundation warns about.
5. **Captions are part of the photograph.** A name, a place, an hour. Editorial
   authority comes from the specificity of the caption as much as the frame.

### The blocker that outranks all of this

**Eighty-four Unsplash images across all twenty pages.** The site's own About
page says *"we would rather show you real ones than borrowed ones,"* and
Foundation §18 forbids stock people standing in for hosts and guests.

No amount of environmental design will make a page of stock photography feel
like being welcomed by friends. **Real photography is the highest-leverage
work in Phase 2 and outranks every SVG, background and motion decision in this
document.** The CMS path for it is already built and waiting.

## 7. Colour Philosophy

The palette is not changing. Tokens already exist:
`--pap-yellow` #FFB81C · `--pap-charcoal` #1A1A1A · `--pap-cream` #FFF7E8 ·
`--pap-forest` #123F35 · `--pap-terracotta` #C2553D · `--pap-soft-gold` #F2D27A.

What Phase 2 defines is **when each appears emotionally**.

| Colour | Emotional role | Appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Charcoal** | Composure, focus, seriousness | Ground for photography; heritage; the booking flow |
| **Cream** | Welcome, daylight, ease | Openings and invitations; where the page should exhale |
| **Yellow** | Recognition — the brand's raised hand | Sparingly. One decisive moment per view |
| **Soft gold** | Warmth without insistence | Where yellow would shout: large fields, gradients, light |
| **Forest** | Depth, shade, interior calm | Nature and craft; a second dark that is not black |
| **Terracotta** | Earth, making, human touch | Craft and food; accents only |

### Three rules

1. **Yellow is a verb.** It marks the single most important action in view.
   Two yellows competing on one screen means neither is chosen.
2. **Warmth belongs to light and photography, not to fills.** Large warm
   blocks are the fastest route to the tourism-brochure register.
3. **Yellow fails accessibility as text on a light surface** (1.73:1). Use
   `--yellow-on-light` for words on cream or white. Yellow as a *fill* is
   fine anywhere. This is a hard constraint, not a preference.

## 8. Ghanaian Design Principles

The brief is right that Adinkra should inform composition rather than be
applied as icons. Taken seriously, that means each symbol contributes a
*structural* idea:

**Sankofa — return to retrieve.** The most important, and it is a *narrative
structure*, not a bird. It means the site is built to be revisited: a page
should reward a second reading, and looking back should feel natural rather
than nostalgic. Practically, it argues for content that deepens on return and
against anything that expires or pressures.

**Nkyinkyim — the twisting path.** Adaptability. Argues against rigid
symmetry and lockstep grids, and *for* the site's asymmetric layouts, which
already exist and should be trusted further. It is also the philosophical
argument for the house easing curve: arrival by a path, not a straight line.

**Aya — endurance.** Restraint that ages. Chooses the treatment that will look
composed in three years over the one that looks current this season.

**Duafe — care, cleanliness, attention.** The craft standard: alignment,
consistent spacing, a considered caption. Care is legible.

**Fawohodie — independence.** The brand is not a supplicant to travel-industry
conventions. No countdown timers, no scarcity language, no borrowed
booking-engine patterns.

### Additional sources, used structurally

- **Coastline** — the horizon as the organising horizontal.
- **Textile** — *rhythm*, specifically the strip-weave logic of repeating
  units with deliberate irregularity. This should influence layout cadence.
  It should never become printed cloth pattern in a background.
- **Architecture** — courtyards, shade, thresholds: the idea that arrival is
  gradual and there is somewhere to sit.
- **Market** — density that is legible because it is organised by use. Argues
  for generous but structured information.

## 9. Diaspora Storytelling Principles

1. **Acknowledge, do not announce.** The strongest signal is a sentence that
   assumes return is normal. Contact already does this well: *"whether this
   will be your first time in Ghana or a long-awaited return."* That register
   is the model.
2. **Never make heritage a product category.** A "Roots Package" reduces a
   personal reckoning to a tier. Heritage is context throughout, not a SKU.
3. **Leave room for grief.** Cape Coast and Assin Manso are places where
   people weep. The design duty is space, quiet and an unhurried pace — and
   the operational promise already made in Foundation §10: *"we allow room for
   reflection at emotionally significant sites."* The site should say so
   plainly.
4. **Contemporary Ghana carries equal weight.** A returner is not only meeting
   ancestry; they are meeting a country with a music scene, a design scene and
   opinions about jollof. Showing only heritage is its own flattening.
5. **Never speak for them.** The site should not tell a returner what they
   will feel. It offers the door and the context; the meaning is theirs.

**Where this belongs:** homepage hero and pathways, the About origin story,
Cape Coast / Elmina / Assin Manso detail pages, and the booking reassurance.
Four or five sentences placed precisely — not a rewrite.

## 10. Design Rules

1. Photography leads. Decoration supports or is removed.
2. Environmental density is inverse to emotional weight.
3. Nothing decorative near sites of memory.
4. The booking flow stays plain.
5. One yellow moment per view.
6. Motion is scroll-driven or triggered, never ambient and looping.
7. Every effect ships with its reduced-motion design.
8. Negative space is a deliberate element, not leftover.
9. Specificity beats superlative, in image and in copy.
10. Use the semantic tokens; the design system is the vocabulary.
11. If a decoration's removal would not be noticed, it should not ship.
12. Claims stay inside the Claim Register. Emotional claims too.

---

## 11. Things We Will Never Do

- Decorate heritage or memorial content.
- Use Adinkra symbols as pattern, wallpaper, bullets or dividers.
- Use kente or printed cloth as a background texture.
- Use stock photography of people as hosts, guests, founders or partners.
- Say "discover," "exotic," "hidden gem," "undiscovered" or "bucket list."
- Use scarcity, countdowns or pressure devices of any kind.
- Animate anything perpetually in the periphery.
- Put text over an image without a legibility treatment.
- Let a decorative element reduce contrast below AA.
- Flatten Ghana into colour, wildlife, poverty or spectacle.
- Tell a returner what they should feel.
- Add a visual flourish to compensate for a weak photograph. Replace the
  photograph.

## 12. Future SVG System Direction

*Direction only. No assets in Phase 2A.*

**Purpose:** structural and atmospheric, never illustrative. The system should
be able to build horizons, layered depth, soft masks and edge transitions —
and should not be able to draw huts, drums, wildlife or symbols.

**Character:** cut-paper rather than drawn. Clean edges, flat planes, implied
depth through overlap rather than rendering. It should look *made* — which is
the honest translation of craft — without imitating any specific craft.

**Constraints:** inline and themeable by token, never a raster; sub-2KB per
element; every form must have a still state; nothing that carries meaning may
live only in an SVG, since it is decorative to a screen reader by definition.

**The test:** if an element could be swapped for a different one without
changing what the page means, it is correctly decorative. If it *carries*
meaning, it belongs in HTML instead.

---

## 13. Future Background System Direction

*Direction only.*

Four registers matching the density gradient in §4 — **Open, Ambient, Quiet,
Silent** — expressed as a small, composable set rather than bespoke artwork
per section. Any page should be assembled from them.

**Rules:** one register per section, never blended. Registers are assigned by
emotional weight, not by variety. Silent is a legitimate and frequent choice.
Backgrounds are theme-driven from tokens so light and dark sections share one
system. Nothing scroll-jacks; nothing loads eagerly above a photograph.

**Success looks like:** a visitor who could not describe the backgrounds
afterwards, but who found the site calm.

## 14. Editorial Storytelling Principles

Foundation §19 defines the *territories*. This defines the **form**.

1. **One idea per section.** If a band needs two headings, it is two bands.
2. **The caption is a first-class citizen** — often the most-read text on the
   page, and where specificity and authority live.
3. **Vary the measure deliberately.** A narrow column reads as intimate, a
   wide one as expansive. Changing it is a storytelling decision.
4. **Let the page breathe between beats.** The pause carries the emphasis.
5. **Openings earn attention; they do not demand it.** Begin with the
   particular, not the grand claim.
6. **Type carries the register.** Which brings us to the outstanding debt:
   **typography is not yet systematised** — 199 raw sizes across 49 distinct
   values, and no scale. Editorial design is impossible without one. **A type
   scale is the first implementation task of Phase 2B**, ahead of any
   background or SVG work.

## 15. Success Criteria

The governing question, applied to every decision:

> **Does this make visitors feel more welcomed, more connected, and more at
> home in Ghana?**

Supporting tests:

- **The removal test.** Take it away. If nothing is lost, it should not ship.
- **The returner test.** Would someone visiting their ancestral home for the
  first time feel addressed, or feel like a tourist?
- **The friend test.** Would a person who lives in Accra recognise this as how
  they would introduce their home — or wince?
- **The quiet test.** Could a visitor describe the decoration afterwards? If
  yes, it was too loud.
- **The still test.** Does it hold up with motion disabled?
- **The grief test.** Applied near heritage: would this feel appropriate to
  someone in tears?

### What "done" looks like for Phase 2

Not a more decorated website. A website where **the photography is real**, the
returner is addressed **on the homepage rather than in an FAQ**, the type
carries the editorial register, and the environment is felt without being
noticed.

If Phase 2 delivered only the first two of those and no SVG system at all, it
would still be the more successful outcome.

---

## Sequencing recommendation for Phase 2B

Ordered by contribution to the success criteria, not by appetite:

1. **Type scale.** Everything editorial depends on it.
2. **Real photography** through the CMS path already built.
3. **Diaspora messaging** in the four or five identified places.
4. **Elevation/shadow scale.** The remaining Phase 1 token debt.
5. **Fix the carousel's reduced-motion defect.**
6. **Background system**, starting with Silent and Quiet.
7. **SVG system**, last and smallest.

The order matters: items 1–3 change how the site feels. Items 6–7 change how
it looks. Doing the latter first would decorate an unfinished foundation.
